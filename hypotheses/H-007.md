## Rationale
DPR toxicity is mechanistically strong but has no pharmacological countermeasure in humans; WVE-004 attacks upstream transcripts (PMID 35592494) with benefit still unproven. The gap: no cheap way to find small molecules that protect cells FROM DPR damage. Genotype-defined iPSC datasets (GSE303931 isogenic C9orf72 lines) make the DPR-induced signature computable, and if the signature concentrates on nucleolar stress and speckle disruption (per PMIDs 36931278, 39181135), then LINCS reversal against THAT signature - rather than generic ALS bulk tissue - is a principled DPR-countermeasure screen. Crucially the same module doubles as a pharmacodynamic readout: any candidate's activity could later be verified by module-score reduction in treated C9orf72 patient neurons, satisfying the synthesis rule that reversal hits must fuse to functional anchors.
