## Rationale
Mitochondrial and axonal-transport defects are real and reproducible in ALS (sec 1.7) but the pathway's first big clinical bet (CNM-Au8, generic catalytic support) missed its primary endpoint (PMID 40067821). We hypothesize the tractable version is narrower: genetically-anchored transport machinery (KIF5A, DCTN1-adjacent nodes) defines a patient submodule with measurable transcriptional dysregulation in human motor neurons, that this module tracks progression speed, and that specific druggable nodes inside it - not global energetics - can be reversed by existing perturbagens. Success would convert a 'moderate-strong, causally unclear' mechanism row into a stratified, target-level hypothesis; failure localizes why the pathway keeps failing clinically.
